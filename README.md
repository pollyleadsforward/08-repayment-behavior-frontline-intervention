# Product Intervention Beyond the System
## Combining Repayment Automation with Customer Education & Frontline Execution

> Solving a repayment problem through both system intervention and customer-behavior intervention rather than relying on technology alone.

**Case Study | 2023**

**Product Strategy · Customer Behavior · Frontline Enablement · Customer Education · Repayment Journey · Cross-Functional Execution**

---

## Executive Summary

While improving the repayment mechanism through automated retry, we identified another important customer behavior.

Some business-owner customers had sufficient cash flow to make their loan repayments.

However, the funds in their accounts were often reused for day-to-day business operations.

By the time the scheduled direct debit occurred, the available balance could therefore become insufficient.

This revealed an important limitation:

> **Improving the repayment system alone would not fully solve the repayment problem.**

At the time, I was also responsible for outbound sales activities.

Rather than treating repayment as an issue owned only by collection or technology teams, I worked with Sales to introduce a customer-education intervention directly into the frontline journey.

The solution combined:

**System Automation + Customer Education + Frontline Execution**

---

## The Customer-Behavior Gap

The technical repayment process could be improved through automated retry.

But the underlying customer behavior remained important.

For business owners:

**Cash Enters Account**

↓

**Funds Are Reused for Business Operations**

↓

**Scheduled Repayment Occurs Later**

↓

**Available Balance May No Longer Be Sufficient**

This meant that a purely technical solution would address only part of the problem.

The broader product question became:

> **What if the system works correctly, but customer behavior still prevents the desired outcome?**

---

## Expanding the Product Boundary

Instead of viewing repayment as only a system or collection problem, I expanded the solution into the customer interaction journey.

I collaborated with Sales to help customers better understand how repayment worked.

Sales representatives supported customers in understanding:

- How to access their loan account
- How to check upcoming repayment dates
- When scheduled direct debit would take place
- The importance of preparing sufficient funds before the repayment date
- The need to maintain the required account balance until the debit was completed

This connected:

**Product → Sales → Customer Education**

rather than relying solely on system automation.

---

## Two-Layer Intervention

The repayment problem was addressed through two complementary interventions.

### 1. System Intervention

Automated retry increased the opportunity to collect repayment when sufficient funds became available.

### 2. Behavioral Intervention

Customer education helped customers:

- Understand when repayment would occur
- Prepare sufficient funds
- Avoid unintentionally using funds required for repayment before the scheduled debit

Together, the two interventions addressed:

**System Limitation + Customer-Behavior Gap**

---

## Why Technology Alone Was Not Enough

A common product response to an operational problem is to build another feature.

But another system enhancement was not necessarily the only answer.

The key insight was:

> **The outcome depended on both system behavior and customer behavior.**

If the system became more capable but customers still did not understand the repayment mechanics, part of the problem would remain.

The solution therefore needed to influence both.

---

## Frontline Teams as Part of the Product Experience

Sales representatives were not treated only as a distribution channel.

They became part of the product experience.

The frontline team helped customers understand how to use the lending product successfully after origination.

This shifted the role of Sales from:

> **Acquire the Customer**

toward:

> **Acquire + Educate + Enable Successful Product Usage**

For financial products, this can be particularly important when customer behavior directly affects repayment outcomes.

---

## Cross-Functional Execution

The initiative connected three areas:

### Product

Identified the customer-behavior gap and defined the intervention.

### Sales

Integrated the education into frontline customer conversations.

### Customer

Received clearer guidance on how repayment worked and what action was required.

The operating model became:

**Product Insight**

↓

**Frontline Guidance**

↓

**Customer Understanding**

↓

**Better Repayment Preparedness**

This allowed the business to address a problem that system automation alone could not fully solve.

---

## Product Thinking Behind the Decision

The core lesson was not simply that Product should collaborate with Sales.

It was that product managers need to identify **where the real cause of an outcome sits**.

The problem could have been framed as:

> “The direct debit system needs another enhancement.”

Instead, the broader diagnosis was:

**Part Technology Problem + Part Customer-Behavior Problem**

That led to a different solution architecture.

---

## Outcome

The combined approach proved effective in practice by addressing the repayment issue from two directions:

- Automated retry provided additional collection opportunities.
- Customer education improved understanding of repayment timing and expected customer action.

Most importantly, the initiative demonstrated that improving a financial product does not always require another technology feature.

Sometimes the better intervention is to connect:

> **Product Design + Customer Behavior + Frontline Execution**

---

## Product Leadership Lessons

This case reinforced an important product principle:

> **Do not assume every customer problem requires a system solution.**

Before building another feature, I ask:

- Is this a technology problem?
- Is this a process problem?
- Is this a customer-understanding problem?
- Is this a behavioral problem?
- Can the frontline influence the outcome?
- Would a combination of interventions work better than technology alone?

The strongest solution may sit across several of these dimensions.
